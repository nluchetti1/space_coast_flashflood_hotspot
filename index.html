Hot spots active at this threshold

    How to read this. Each hot spot is a cluster of NWS flood / flash-flood reports. For each report, the build finds the peak Stage IV accumulation for each duration (1–72 h) over windows ending between 12 h before the report and 2 h after it. A spot's trigger for a duration is the lowest peak that has produced a flood report there. The spot lights up when the threshold is at or above its trigger, meaning "this much rain in this time has flooded here before".

    Caveats. Reports only exist where flooding was seen and reported, so the secured interiors of CCSFS and KSC are under-sampled. Stage IV is about 4.7 km. Hourly fields get less RFC QC than the 6-hourly ones; 48/72 h use 6-hourly files, and values marked † fell back to 6-hourly because of gaps in the hourly data. There's no record of heavy rain that didn't flood a spot, so each trigger is an upper bound on the true threshold. For short durations the peak 1–3 h burst may not be what caused flooding that came after a long soak. Tropical events can mix lagoon or surge water with rainfall flooding.

    Data: IEM LSR archive, NCEI Storm Events, NCEP Stage IV via IEM.
